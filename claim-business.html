Business Ownership

Claim Business

This policy explains how business representatives can claim NearAfrica listings, what information may be required, how claims are reviewed, and what happens when ownership or authorization cannot be established.

1. Purpose of the Claim System

NearAfrica may allow an authorized representative of a business to claim an existing business listing.

The purpose of the claim system is to provide an appropriate process for businesses to identify themselves as the authorized representative of a listing and, where approved, manage eligible business information.

Submitting a claim does not automatically give the claimant control of a business listing.

A claim must be reviewed and accepted before claim-based access or status is granted.

2. Who Can Claim a Business

A claim should only be submitted by a person who is genuinely authorized to represent the business.

Depending on the circumstances, an eligible claimant may include:

  • The business owner.
  • A director, partner, or authorized officer.
  • An authorized manager.
  • An employee authorized to manage the listing.
  • An officially authorized representative or agency.

A customer, competitor, unrelated individual, or other person without authorization should not submit a claim merely because they know information about the business.

3. Claim Process

A typical claim may involve the following process:

Identify the listing

Locate the correct NearAfrica business listing and confirm that it represents the business you are authorized to represent.

Submit claim information

Provide your name, contact information, role, relationship to the business, and other requested information.

Provide evidence when requested

NearAfrica may request information reasonably necessary to establish authorization.

Review

NearAfrica may review the claim and compare the information with the listing and available evidence.

Decision

The claim may be approved, rejected, or placed on hold while additional information is requested.

4. Verification Evidence

NearAfrica may use reasonable evidence to determine whether a claimant appears authorized to represent a business.

Depending on the circumstances, this may include:

  • A business email address.
  • A business telephone number.
  • Information already displayed on the business listing.
  • Information supplied through another established business channel.
  • Evidence of a relationship with the business.
  • Business registration or other official information where reasonably necessary.
  • Other evidence reasonably relevant to the claim.

NearAfrica may use more than one verification method when the circumstances require additional confidence.

5. Business Email Verification

Where appropriate, NearAfrica may use an email address associated with the business to help assess a claim.

Possession of an email address alone does not necessarily establish legal ownership or authority.

NearAfrica may request additional evidence where an email address does not provide sufficient assurance.

6. Telephone Verification

NearAfrica may use a business telephone number or another appropriate communication method as part of claim verification.

Telephone verification may help establish a connection with the business but does not necessarily prove legal ownership by itself.

7. Supporting Documents

In some cases, NearAfrica may request supporting documentation where other verification methods are insufficient.

Examples may include documents or records that reasonably demonstrate the claimant's relationship with the business.

Do not send unnecessary sensitive personal information.

If NearAfrica requests documentation, provide only information reasonably necessary for the stated verification purpose.

NearAfrica may reject documents that appear altered, misleading, fraudulent, irrelevant, or insufficient.

8. Claim Review

NearAfrica may consider multiple factors when reviewing a claim.

These may include:

  • Whether the claimed listing matches the business.
  • Whether the claimant's information is consistent.
  • Whether the claimant has a reasonable connection to the business.
  • Whether submitted evidence appears authentic.
  • Whether another person has already claimed the listing.
  • Whether the claim appears suspicious or abusive.

NearAfrica may request additional information before making a decision.

9. Claim Statuses

A claim may have different statuses during the review process.

Pending

The claim has been submitted and is awaiting review or additional information.

Approved

NearAfrica has accepted the claim based on the information and verification available.

Rejected

The claim was not accepted because authorization could not be sufficiently established or another applicable issue was identified.

10. What Happens After Approval

If a claim is approved, NearAfrica may associate the claimant with the relevant business listing.

Depending on the features available at the time, an approved representative may be able to:

  • Request or manage eligible listing information.
  • Provide updated business details.
  • Add or manage eligible business media.
  • Respond to certain reviews or reports.
  • Access other business-management features provided by NearAfrica.

Approval does not transfer ownership of the business to NearAfrica or transfer ownership of intellectual property belonging to the business.

11. Rejected Claims

A claim may be rejected where NearAfrica cannot reasonably establish that the claimant is authorized to represent the business.

Other reasons may include:

  • Insufficient evidence.
  • Conflicting information.
  • Suspected impersonation.
  • Suspected fraud.
  • Duplicate or conflicting claims.
  • Inability to verify the submitted information.

A rejected claimant may contact NearAfrica with additional relevant information where appropriate.

12. Fraudulent or Unauthorized Claims

Attempting to claim a business that you do not legitimately represent is prohibited.

NearAfrica may take action where a claim appears fraudulent or intentionally misleading.

Depending on the circumstances, this may include:

  • Rejecting the claim.
  • Restricting the claimant's access.
  • Removing associated content.
  • Cancelling improperly granted access.
  • Preserving relevant records.
  • Reporting suspected unlawful conduct to an appropriate authority where legally required or appropriate.

Never submit a claim simply to gain control of another business's listing.

13. Competing Claims and Disputes

More than one person may sometimes claim to represent the same business.

When competing claims occur, NearAfrica may place the claim or listing on hold while the matter is reviewed.

NearAfrica may request additional evidence from one or more claimants.

NearAfrica's internal claim decision does not determine legal ownership of a company, business, trademark, property, or other asset.

Where a dispute concerns legal ownership or rights that NearAfrica cannot reasonably determine, NearAfrica may decline to decide the underlying legal dispute and may take temporary administrative action concerning the listing.

14. Account and Claim Security

Claimants are responsible for protecting their account credentials and information used to access NearAfrica features.

You should not:

  • Share passwords or authentication codes.
  • Allow unauthorized people to use your account.
  • Attempt to bypass NearAfrica's verification procedures.
  • Attempt to access another claimant's account.
  • Submit false information to obtain access.

Suspected unauthorized access should be reported to NearAfrica as soon as reasonably possible.

15. Personal Information Used During Claims

Claim processing may require NearAfrica to collect personal information such as a claimant's name, email address, telephone number, role, and verification information.

Such information may be used to:

  • Process and verify the claim.
  • Communicate with the claimant.
  • Prevent fraudulent claims.
  • Resolve disputes.
  • Maintain administrative and security records.
  • Comply with applicable legal obligations.

Personal information is handled according to the NearAfrica Privacy Policy and applicable law.

NearAfrica may retain limited claim records after a claim is resolved where reasonably necessary for security, legal, dispute-resolution, or audit purposes.

16. Revocation or Loss of Claim Access

Claim-based access may be revoked or restricted if:

  • The original claim is discovered to have been fraudulent.
  • The claimant is no longer authorized to represent the business.
  • The account is compromised.
  • The claimant violates NearAfrica policies.
  • Material information used to approve the claim is found to be false or misleading.
  • Legal or security considerations require action.

NearAfrica may request updated evidence when continued authorization is reasonably in question.

17. Limitations of the Claim System

NearAfrica's claim system is an administrative verification process. It is not a court proceeding and does not determine legal ownership of a business.

Approval does not guarantee that:

  • The claimant is legally entitled to every business asset.
  • The business is properly licensed.
  • The business complies with every applicable law.
  • The information on the listing is permanently accurate.
  • The business is endorsed or recommended by NearAfrica.

NearAfrica may update its verification procedures as the platform develops and as new risks or verification methods arise.

18. Claim Support

Questions about claims, verification, competing claims, unauthorized access, or suspected fraudulent claims can be reported to NearAfrica.
